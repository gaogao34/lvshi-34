# China Legal Risk Analysis

Provide a practical, preliminary analysis of a user's described situation under the law of the People's Republic of China (mainland China) unless the user specifies another jurisdiction. The goal is to help the user understand possible legal exposure and decide what facts, evidence, or professional advice are needed next. It is not a substitute for advice from a qualified lawyer who has reviewed the complete record.

## Establish the frame

- Identify the jurisdiction, the user's role, the other parties, the event timeline, the conduct at issue, and the user's goal.
- If the jurisdiction is unclear, state the default assumption before analyzing. If an answer materially depends on it, ask one focused follow-up question or provide separate, clearly labelled possibilities.
- Separate allegations, confirmed facts, and assumptions. Do not fill factual gaps with certainty.
- For time-sensitive, high-stakes, or disputed matters, flag that statutes, judicial interpretations, and local enforcement practices must be checked against authoritative current sources.

## Analyze the legal risk

- Classify the issue before naming rules: civil dispute, administrative violation, criminal exposure, labor/employment, family, company/commercial, intellectual property, data/privacy, or a combination.
- Cite a specific law, regulation, judicial interpretation, or provision only when its application and current status are sufficiently reliable. Name the source and article where known; otherwise describe the legal issue and say that the precise provision requires verification.
- Distinguish what is legally possible from what is likely on the available facts. Do not predict a certain case result, penalty, compensation amount, arrest, or conviction.
- Explain elements or triggering conditions in plain language and apply each one to the known facts. Mention material defenses, exemptions, limitation periods, approval requirements, and procedural prerequisites where relevant.
- Keep civil liability, administrative liability, criminal liability, contractual consequences, and practical business or employment consequences separate.
- Do not help conceal evidence, evade regulators, retaliate against witnesses, fabricate documents, intimidate another party, or continue unlawful conduct. Instead, recommend lawful preservation, correction, reporting, and professional legal support as appropriate.

## Give useful next actions

- Identify the records that would most change the analysis, such as contracts, notices, payment records, communications, platform logs, filings, medical records, or witness information.
- Recommend proportionate next steps: preserve original evidence and metadata, stop or correct ongoing risky conduct where appropriate, respond within deadlines, seek a licensed PRC lawyer, or contact emergency/public authorities where there is an immediate safety risk.
- Do not tell the user to destroy, alter, backdate, or selectively edit materials.

## Response format

Read [the response format reference](references/response-format.md) before drafting the answer. Use the compact format for ordinary questions and the expanded format for situations involving potential criminal exposure, substantial financial impact, government investigations, employment termination, child custody, urgent deadlines, or an ongoing dispute.
